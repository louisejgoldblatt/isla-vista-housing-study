# Methodology

## Study Design

The Isla Vista Housing Conditions Study is designed to collect tenant-reported
data on housing conditions across Isla Vista, California. The sampling approach
combines geographic stratification, development of a residential-unit sampling
frame, and random selection to improve geographic coverage and reduce selection
bias.

## 1. Geographic Stratification

Isla Vista was divided into 14 geographic sampling zones using land-use and
street maps.

The zones were designed to create manageable geographic strata while accounting
for differences across Isla Vista, including housing density, building type,
landlord clustering, and proximity to the coast.

Residential buildings within each zone were then assigned unique identifiers
(e.g., A1, A2, B1, B2) to connect mapped properties with the housing-unit census.

The working zone maps are available in the `maps/` directory.

## 2. Building and Unit Census

A residential-unit sampling frame was developed for the 14 zones.

For each mapped building, housing information was collected using online
property research and field verification. The process included:

- Matching residential buildings to their corresponding map identifiers
- Researching the number of housing units associated with each property
- Conducting in-person verification of mapped buildings
- Recording uncertain or conflicting information for additional review
- Quality-checking completed zone records

This process was designed to produce a more complete list of residential units
from which a randomized sample could be drawn.

## 3. Sampling Frame

After the zone census was completed, residential-unit records were organized
into a single sampling frame and assigned to their corresponding geographic
zones.

The resulting working sampling frame contains approximately 895 residential
units across 14 geographic strata.

## 4. Stratified Random Sampling

Rather than drawing a single random sample from all of Isla Vista, sampling is
conducted within each geographic zone.

The target sample is allocated across zones based on the number of housing
units in each stratum. Units are then randomly selected within their respective
zones without replacement.

This approach is intended to maintain random selection while improving
geographic representation across Isla Vista.

## 5. Nonresponse Protocol

Primary housing units are randomly selected before survey outreach begins.

Additional units are also randomized in advance to create reserve selections.
If a primary unit cannot participate, the next predetermined reserve unit can
be used rather than allowing field researchers to select a convenient
replacement.

This procedure is intended to reduce convenience-sampling bias during data
collection.

## 6. Planned Analysis

Following survey collection, responses will be analyzed at an aggregated level
to examine patterns in tenant-reported housing conditions across Isla Vista.

Potential analyses include comparisons across geographic areas and housing
characteristics, as well as exploratory spatial analysis of housing-condition
patterns.

## Project Status

The study is currently in progress. Sampling design and survey development have
been completed or are underway, while survey collection and subsequent analysis
remain future stages of the project.

To protect the integrity of the ongoing study, unit-level sampling information
and selected residential addresses are not included in this public repository.
